# Nonlinear-and-photocurrent-dynamics-of-K-and-Cl-optogenetics
Nonlinear and photocurrent dynamics underlying superior suppression of epileptiform bursting by K^{+} versus Cl^{−} optogenetics
Inhibitory optogenetics holds therapeutic promise for brain disorders, suppressing abnormal firing through coupling between photocurrent and neuron. In recent experiments, conventional Cl− optogenetics paradoxically provokes epileptiform bursting along with diseases rather than suppressing it, whereas the novel K+ optogenetics suppresses such activity. In this study, focusing on constant optogenetic stimulation, two methods are developed to overcome the difficulty of bifurcation analysis induced by time-varying photocurrent stimulation, and the mechanisms underlying these opposite outcomes are then investigated using a neuron model incorporating dynamic Cl− and K+ concentrations. First, a direct method reveals the bifurcations that separate the opposite responses at large optogenetic conductance, which are further explained by an indirect method: the K+ and Cl− photocur-rents, both crossing the bifurcation threshold of the isolated neuron, act as strong constant inhibition inducing a resting state and weak pulsed inhibition triggering paradoxical bursting, respectively. Increases in conductance induce a more negative photocur-rent pulse, longer bursting latency, and more depolarized membrane voltage. Second, the modulation mechanism of weak Cl− photocurrent pulse at large conductance is elucidated. The Cl− photocurrent strengthens more slowly than the conductance, i.e., sublinearly, resulting in a current too weak to induce a resting state, whereas the sublinear but stronger K+ photocurrent can. This difference arises because the Cl− driving force weakens more than the K+ driving force: the former from a less negative reversal potential and a more depolarized membrane potential, the latter from a nearly constant reversal potential and a more hyperpolarized membrane potential. Third, two additional experimentally observed phenomena in K+ optogenetics, a prolonged bursting latency and a resting state, are reproduced with increasing optogenetic stimulation intensity, following a principle resembling that in the conductance case. These findings provide theoretical guidance for selecting appropriate optogenetic approaches and parameters to suppress pathological firing in the treatment of brain disorders.
